❓ Comprehensive Q&A Register Explorer

Complete verified answers to all 12 core analytical questions and 5 strategic DTCC candidate discussion questions.

Q-01: Which factors are associated with higher reconciliation-break risk? +

Answer: The primary statistical risk drivers in order of empirical impact are:

  1. mapping_version_age_days: Stale account mapping tables (>90d old) are the strongest predictor (drop in PR-AUC of ~0.12 when shuffled; Odds Ratio: 2.84x).
  2. vendor_dup_flag: Duplicate vendor and amount within 7 days (Odds Ratio: 3.82x, p < 0.0001).
  3. is_manual_entry: Manual journals carry more than double the break odds of automated feeds (Odds Ratio: 2.21x).
  4. close_manual_interaction: Manual postings within 3 days of close compound risk significantly.
Q-03: Which model performs best against the existing analyst heuristic? +

Answer: Logistic Regression decisively beats the 3-rule heuristic: achieving 0.251 PR-AUC vs. 0.179 for the heuristic, and capturing 33.2% of all breaks (157 / 473) in the top 10% volume versus only 21.8% for the heuristic (3.32x vs 2.18x lift). This finds 1.52x more problems with the exact same analyst capacity.

Q-04: Does a more complex model outperform logistic regression by a meaningful amount? +

Answer: No. Random Forest achieved 0.237 PR-AUC (30.7% capture) and Gradient Boosting achieved 0.208 PR-AUC (29.4% capture), compared to Logistic Regression at 0.251 PR-AUC (33.2% capture). The models operate within sampling noise (+/- 3.5%). Logistic regression is recommended because controllers and regulators can inspect its exact odds ratios.

Q-08: What is the business impact of refreshing stale mapping tables? +

Answer: In Scenario F simulation, enforcing a mandatory 60-day Chart-of-Accounts mapping table refresh SLA reduced systemic predicted break risk across the portfolio by 46.7% and prevented hundreds of transactions from escalating into the review queue. Upstream governance prevents more breaks than downstream detection can resolve.
